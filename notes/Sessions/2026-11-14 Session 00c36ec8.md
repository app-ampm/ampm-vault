## Narrative

The user asked why app.ampmverse.com/sign-in looks like a phone screen on a desktop. Cause: from 768px the sign-in renders one centred `max-w-md` (448px) card with everything stacked inside, while the D1 artboard is a 1440 two-column split (headline left, 520px glass form card right). The user then asked for a thorough hunt for every similar problem so they can all be fixed in one go.

The worktree branch was merged up to `origin/main` (merge 0357833; only graphify cache conflicts). The local API (3900, DB `ampm_peacock`) and Metro (8082) were started, and every built route was rendered at 1440, 768 and 390 with headless Playwright installed in the scratchpad, signed in as client, prospect, sales and admin. Three read-only audit agents compared the screens and the shell with the D, T and P artboards; the sign-in/portal agent was stopped and that scope was covered from the renders. Findings were checked against the renders.

Result, written to `docs/planning/desktop-layout-audit.md`: tablet and phone are mostly fine. On desktop almost every screen keeps its phone composition. Root causes: client pages are capped at `Screen width="content"` (768, about 672 of content), which guide §7 mandates while contradicting its own breakpoint table (rule from PR #98, no D-record); staff `wide` is capped at 1152 and centred; there are no shared Columns, SplitPane or MasterDetail layouts; primitives use phone sizes; the navigation waits for `/me` (layout shift); the glow is drawn per Screen, so the glass navigation is flat. The audit has per-screen fixes for D1, D2, D4–D8 and T2–T6, T12 and M11, the phone bugs (hotel filter truncation, catalogue header overflow) and five open decisions (guide amendment, D5 map interim, glow tones, pipeline closed columns, rail sections). Nothing is fixed yet; the next step is the shared layer, then the client screens, then the staff screens.

At the user's request the sign-in/portal/account audit was re-run. Its findings were spot-checked against the mocks and the code, and merged into the audit doc: exact D1 values for the sign-in split; D2 as `1fr 1.1fr` columns; D4's `1.5fr 1fr` and `1fr 1fr 0.9fr` rows with compact My selection rows; `/account` stretched across 1056; and two new shared causes, R8 (`/portal` loads at 768, then jumps to `wide`) and R9 (no shared split sign-in layout, no hero header size, missing glow placements).

The user then approved the shared layer and the guide amendment, and it was built and committed (6731d2c, [[D-123 Desktop screens follow the D, C and T artboards  frame-aware Screen widths and shared column layouts|D-123]]):
- **`Screen`:** reads a `ScreenFrameProvider` frame. Beside the rail, `wide` fills the window with 18/30/30 gutters. Under the portal bar, `wide` uses 72 and the new `split` 32, inside a 1440 `max-w-frame`. The tab-bar room is phone-only.
- **New layout components:** `Columns`, `SplitPane`, `MasterDetail` and `Grid` in `@ampm/ui`.
- **`ScreenHeader`:** titles at 44 from 1024, and a `hero` size.
- **Tokens:** spacing `7.5` and `18`; sizes `list` and `frame`; `maxWidth` takes the sizes.
- **Navigation:** the rail and the portal bar now float over the screens (the screens' safe-area insets hold their room), so the per-screen glow shows through the glass. They draw their frame from the route before `/me` answers.
- **`/portal`:** loads at the subscriber page's width.
- **Guide:** §6, §7 and §10 are amended; the client 768 rule (PR #98, never recorded) is gone.

Window-level glow was rejected: screens pick their own glow, and the web stack keeps earlier screens mounted. Global card padding steps were skipped because they would override screens' own `md:p-*`.

Checks: `pnpm check` is green (642 UI and 743 app tests) and so is the format check. Before and after renders at 390 are pixel-identical. At 768 and 1440, staff pages and client `wide` pages take the new gutters.

Remaining: phase 2, the client screens (D1 with the shared split sign-in layout, D2, D4–D8); phase 3, the staff screens (T2–T6, T12, M11); the phone fixes; and decisions on the D5 map interim, the glow tones, the pipeline's closed columns and the rail sections.
